% ===========================================================================
\section{Conclusion}
% ===========================================================================

An election result is acted on at once and never reconciled, so a mis-stated
tally has no later event to expose it. Auditing the operator does not fix that.
It moves trust to the auditor, and to records the operator produced.

\projectname{} puts the rules that decide an election into contract code on a
public testbed: who may vote, when phases change, and which ballots count. A
reader who disbelieves the result recomputes it from the raw log. The nullifier
that stops a second ballot never reveals whose it was.

Anonymity rests on a Merkle commitment rather than Semaphore's on-chain group
management, which would charge a transaction and a leaf insertion per voter.
Only the root, the roll size and the leaf set's IPFS identifier reach the chain,
and the booth derives its own authentication path from the published leaf set.
The constant-cost claim is per operation, not per election: total gas still
grows with ballots cast, and the roll-sized work moved onto the registrar, the
auditors and the booth.

The gaps in Section~3 are carried here as future work. Eligibility rests on a
trusted setup this group did not run; we verify the hashes of Semaphore's
published ceremony artifacts, but collusion by every contributor would let
forged proofs verify undetectably. Roll correctness is not guaranteed, because
one registrar decides who is enrolled. Publishing the roll size does not make
stuffing detectable; it only commits the capacity to stuff before any ballot
exists. Fixing that needs the registrar split across parties, or observers
co-signing the leaf set before the root freezes.

Three more limits stand. Coercion and credential sale need MACI-style key
rotation. Secrecy from the operator holds only while the relayer runs separately
from the registrar, or the voter submits directly, because the relayer reads the
choice and the source IP and can drop ballots naming one candidate. Tally
secrecy during voting needs commit-reveal or homomorphic tallying. So
\projectname{} removes the operator from the ballot box and from the count, and
leaves one party in control of the franchise.

Nothing here has been measured. Evaluation 2 replaces these expectations with
numbers against the parameters in Section~5. C1 measures registration and ballot
gas from ten voters to a hundred thousand; tier 3 should be flat, and above tier
2 throughout, which is the price of anonymity rather than a saving. C2 and C5
guard the flank that leaves open: proving time against tree depth, and whether a
hundred-thousand-leaf roll rebuilds in a browser. A3 prices the A1 gas figure
under three fee regimes as a band. D5 needs no graph at all: with our backend
switched off mid-demonstration, the audit dashboard must still read the chain
and recompute the tally. Missed targets will be reported too.
