% ===========================================================================
\section{Conclusion}
% ===========================================================================

An election result is acted on at once, never reconciled, so a mis-stated
tally has no later event to expose it. An audit only moves trust to the auditor
and to records the operator produced. \projectname{} instead puts the rules
deciding an election into contract code on a public testbed: who may vote, when
phases change, which ballots count. A reader who disbelieves the result
recomputes it from the raw log, and the nullifier that stops a second ballot
never reveals whose it was. Anonymity rests on a Merkle commitment rather than
Semaphore's on-chain group management, which charges a transaction and a leaf
insertion per voter. Only the root, the roll size and the leaf set's IPFS
identifier reach the chain; the booth derives its own authentication path from
it. The constant-cost claim is per operation, not per election. Total gas stays
linear in ballots cast; roll-sized work moved onto the registrar, auditors
and booth.

Section~3's gaps are carried here as future work, with remedies. Eligibility
rests on a trusted setup this group did not run. We verify the hashes of
Semaphore's published ceremony artifacts, but if every contributor colluded,
forged membership proofs would verify undetectably. Roll correctness is
not guaranteed. One registrar decides who is enrolled, and publishing the roll size
does not make stuffing detectable; it commits the capacity to stuff before any
ballot exists. A fix needs the registrar split across parties, or observers
co-signing the leaf set before the root freezes. Coercion and credential sale
need MACI-style key rotation. Ballot secrecy from the operator holds only while
the relayer runs separately from the registrar, or the voter submits directly.
The relayer reads the choice and the source IP, and can drop ballots naming
one candidate. Encrypted ballots or several independent relayers would remove
both; this project builds neither. Tally secrecy during voting needs
commit-reveal or homomorphic tallying, and network-level anonymity needs
anonymizing transport. \projectname{} removes the operator from the ballot box
and from the count, and leaves one party in control of the franchise.

Nothing here has been measured. Evaluation 2 replaces these expectations with
numbers against Section~5's parameters. C1 measures registration and ballot gas
from ten voters to a hundred thousand. Tier 3 should be flat and above tier 2
throughout: the price of anonymity, not a cost win. C2 and C5 guard the flank
it leaves open: proving time against tree depth, whether a hundred-thousand-leaf
roll rebuilds in a browser. A3 prices the A1 gas figure under three fee regimes
as a band, not a point. D5 needs no graph: with our backend off
mid-demonstration, the audit dashboard must still read the chain and recompute
the tally. Missed targets will be reported too.
